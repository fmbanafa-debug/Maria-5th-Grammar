Part 1: Sentences, Commands & Exclamations

Sentences, Fragments, Sentence Types, and End Punctuation

• Sentence: A group of words that expresses a complete thought (starts with capital letter, ends with punctuation).

• Fragment: A group of words that does not express a complete thought (e.g., "Went to the store.")

• Statement: Tells something (ends with a period .).

• Question: Asks something (ends with a question mark ?).

• Command: Tells someone to do something (ends with . or !).

• Exclamation: Expresses strong feeling (ends with !).

A. Decide whether each group of words is a Sentence (S) or a Fragment (F).

B. Read each group of words. Write Statement, Question, Command, or Exclamation.

C. Add the proper punctuation to the end of each sentence. Then write whether it is a Statement, Question, Command, or Exclamation.

D. Do as shown in the brackets (Write your own sentences).

Part 2: Subjects, Predicates & Commas

Simple & Complete Subjects/Predicates, Compound Subjects & Commas

• Simple Subject: Main noun/pronoun naming whom/what the sentence is about.

• Complete Subject: Includes all words telling who/what the sentence is about.

• Simple Predicate: The verb telling exactly what the subject does or is.

• Complete Predicate: The verb and all words telling what the subject does or is.

A. Identify the words or phrases as Subject or Predicate.
